WHY TWO TOTALS CAN DIFFER

The details behind
the dollar amount.

Your restaurant.

Menu prices can vary between locations. A reference from one restaurant or website is useful context, but it does not promise the same price at another address.

Choose your restaurant before you compare the final cost. This also helps you confirm that the items you want are currently available there.

Your changes.

Extra toppings, different sizes and selected substitutions can affect the amount shown at checkout. Read the price again after making changes instead of relying on the original listing.

For a drink, look at both size and flavor. For a meal, review the entrée and its accompaniments as a complete selection.

Your final checkout.

The item subtotal and the final payment are different steps. Taxes and any applicable service or delivery fees may appear later in the ordering process.

Use the last order-review screen to assess the amount you are actually agreeing to pay, including everything added to the basket.

MAKE THE MENU WORK FOR YOU

Plan the order before
you compare the total.

A useful price guide helps you decide what to buy and what to check before paying.

Choose your main priority.

Start with the part of the meal you care about most. That might be a sandwich, several chicken bites or a breakfast item. Choose that first, then decide whether a side and drink belong in the same order.

This approach gives you a clearer budget than adding extras as you scroll. If you already have a drink, compare the individual entrée. If you want a full meal, check the complete combination with the sizes you would actually choose.

Keep sandwich comparisons fair.

Look beyond the headline price when comparing sandwiches. Read the preparation, bread and standard toppings. A grilled sandwich and a breaded sandwich offer different eating experiences, so a small price difference may not be your deciding factor.

Compare the versions you intend to order after any changes. If you add toppings to one sandwich, include them in its total. Otherwise, you are comparing a customized item with a standard item and may miss the difference at checkout.

Read chicken counts carefully.

For bite-sized chicken, check the number of pieces listed with the item. Confirm whether the price belongs to the chicken alone or a meal. A larger count does not automatically mean the order includes a larger side or drink.

When ordering for several people, write down the portions each person wants before comparing options. Separate individual meals and a shared order may suit different groups. Choose around the food everyone will eat rather than the largest number on the menu.

Give breakfast its own budget.

A biscuit, mini sandwiches and a scramble are different ways to build a morning order. Decide which format appeals to you before considering the extras. This makes the breakfast list easier to navigate and keeps the comparison focused.

Review the selected restaurant’s breakfast availability as well as its prices. If you plan to collect later, make sure the items are offered for that window. Keep the drink and side sizes visible when checking a breakfast meal against separate items.

Decide which extras you want.

A side or dessert can be a worthwhile part of your order when it is something you want. Look at each addition separately instead of treating every extra as necessary. Two small additions can change the total more than switching your main item.

For drinks, confirm the size and any selected flavor or additions. When a meal lets you choose between several accompaniments, review the amount after choosing your preferred option. Use the updated basket to assess the order.

Plan group orders clearly.

List each person’s main item, side and drink before building the basket. Keep requested changes beside the relevant name. A short list helps prevent duplicate items and makes it easier to review quantities before payment.

If you are dividing the cost, decide how shared items will be counted. Use the actual receipt once the order is complete. A reference menu is helpful for planning, but the final purchase total is the amount to use for settling payments.

QUESTIONS ABOUT PRICES

Make the numbers
less of a guessing game.

Are the prices on this page guaranteed at every restaurant?+

No. They are dated reference examples from our menu website. The restaurant and ordering service you select provide the price that applies to your purchase.

Does the sandwich price cover a meal?+

Treat the sandwich listing as an individual item unless a meal is explicitly described. A meal may include specific side and drink sizes.

Why does a delivery order cost more than I expected?+

Review the item prices and each added fee in that service. Delivery charges, service fees, taxes and chosen extras can contribute to the final amount.

Can I use the reference list to plan a budget?+

It can help you estimate the scale of an order. Confirm the exact items and local checkout total before treating that estimate as the money you need.

Where did these reference figures come from?+

The sample prices were checked on chickfila-menue.com on October 27, 2026. This independent guide does not claim to represent live nationwide pricing.

Is this a place to purchase food?+

No. This is a menu and price-reading guide. It does not collect payment, transmit orders or reserve products at a restaurant.
